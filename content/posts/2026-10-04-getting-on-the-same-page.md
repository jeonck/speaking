## Today's Idiom

> **on the same page**

This session focuses on the English idiom 'on the same page' and related expressions for talking about shared understanding and agreement. Learners explore how to use these phrases naturally in everyday and professional settings. Common grammar and usage mistakes — such as wrong prepositions and missing articles — are also addressed.

## 💬 Idioms

### “on the same page”

having the same understanding, information, or goals as the people you are working or talking with

- *Let's have a quick meeting to make sure everyone is on the same page before we start.*
- *I thought we had agreed on the budget, but it turned out we weren't on the same page at all.*

### “get on the same page”

to take action — usually through talking or discussing — in order to reach a shared understanding with others

- *The two departments need to get on the same page about the deadline before sending the report.*
- *Can we schedule a quick call to get on the same page about the changes?*

### “be on the same wavelength”

to think in a naturally similar way or to understand each other easily without needing much explanation

- *My new study partner and I are on the same wavelength — we always come up with the same ideas.*
- *It is hard to collaborate with someone when you are not on the same wavelength.*

## 📚 Vocabulary to Remember

- **align** — to bring ideas, goals, or plans into agreement or coordination
  - *We need to align our schedules before we can book the room.*
- **clarify** — to make something clearer or easier to understand
  - *Could you clarify what you mean by 'flexible hours'?*
- **consensus** — general agreement among all or most people in a group
  - *After a long discussion, the team finally reached a consensus on the plan.*
- **miscommunication** — a failure to communicate clearly, which often leads to misunderstandings or mistakes
  - *The wrong date was booked because of a miscommunication between the two teams.*
- **sync up** — (informal) to coordinate with others by sharing updates and making sure everyone has the same information
  - *Let's sync up on Friday to check what everyone has finished this week.*
- **mutual** — shared equally by two or more people; felt or done by both sides
  - *Their strong working relationship is built on mutual trust and open communication.*
- **touch base** — to make brief contact with someone for a quick update or check-in
  - *I'll touch base with my manager after the presentation to get her feedback.*

## 🔧 Say It Better

1. ❌ *We are in the same page.*
   ✅ **We are on the same page.**
   💡 The correct preposition is 'on', not 'in' — picture everyone looking at the same page of a shared document.
2. ❌ *Let's make sure we're on the same pages.*
   ✅ **Let's make sure we're on the same page.**
   💡 'Page' stays singular in this idiom; the image is one shared page, not many.
3. ❌ *I want that we get on the same page before starting.*
   ✅ **I want us to get on the same page before starting.**
   💡 After 'want', use object + infinitive ('want us to do'), not a 'that' clause.
4. ❌ *We should be on same page about this.*
   ✅ **We should be on the same page about this.**
   💡 The definite article 'the' is always required in this idiom and cannot be dropped.
5. ❌ *My colleague and me are not on the same page.*
   ✅ **My colleague and I are not on the same page.**
   💡 When the noun phrase is the subject, use 'I', not 'me' — remove the other name to test: 'I am not on the same page.'
6. ❌ *Before begin the meeting, let's clarify the agenda.*
   ✅ **Before beginning the meeting, let's clarify the agenda.**
   💡 After the preposition 'before', use the gerund (-ing form), not the base verb.
7. ❌ *There was a miscommunicate between us.*
   ✅ **There was a miscommunication between us.**
   💡 'Miscommunication' is the noun; 'miscommunicate' is the verb — choose based on the word's role in the sentence.

## ✅ Check Yourself

**Q1.** The booking error happened because of a ____ — one person said morning and the other heard afternoon.

- miscommunication
- consensus
- mutual

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>miscommunication</strong> — 'Miscommunication' is the noun for a failure to communicate clearly, fitting a mix-up between two people; the other two options are not nouns that name a type of error.</p></details>

**Q2.** Every Monday, the whole team gathers for ten minutes to ____ and share updates before the week begins.

- sync up
- touch base
- clarify

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>sync up</strong> — 'Sync up' means to coordinate as a group and stay aligned — more fitting for a regular full-team ritual than 'touch base', which implies a brief one-on-one check-in.</p></details>

**Q3.** I was surprised to find that a new colleague and I ____ immediately; we had the exact same vision for the project without discussing it.

- were on the same wavelength
- got on the same page
- aligned

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>were on the same wavelength</strong> — 'Were on the same wavelength' describes a natural, effortless connection — unlike 'got on the same page', which implies deliberate effort to reach agreement.</p></details>

**Q4.** The presenter paused and asked, 'Could someone ____ the question? I want to make sure I heard it correctly.'

- clarify
- align
- mutual

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>clarify</strong> — 'Clarify' means to make something easier to understand and works as a verb here; 'align' and 'mutual' don't fit this sentence grammatically or in meaning.</p></details>

**Q5.** After several rounds of debate, the committee finally reached ____ and signed the agreement.

- consensus
- on the same page
- miscommunication

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>consensus</strong> — 'Consensus' is the noun for general agreement and collocates naturally with 'reach'; 'on the same page' is a state, not something you 'reach'.</p></details>

## ✍️ Mini Diary

> Today I had an unexpected coffee chat with a new colleague just before our afternoon meeting. It quickly became clear that we were not **on the same page** about who was supposed to prepare the summary slides — a classic case of **miscommunication** that could have caused real embarrassment. We took about ten minutes to **clarify** our roles, and by the time we walked into the room we had managed to **get on the same page**. The meeting went smoothly, and it reminded me that a short, honest conversation is always worth more than an awkward silence.
