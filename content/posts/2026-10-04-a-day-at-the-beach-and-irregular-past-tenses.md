## Session Overview

A learner described spending a relaxing Saturday at the beach with a friend — walking, talking, and eating ice cream near the pier. The conversation led naturally into a review of irregular simple-past verbs, an area the learner admitted to frequently mixing up. Along the way, expressions like 'give it a try' and 'pick it up quickly' arose while discussing the possibility of trying stand-up paddleboarding in the future.

## 💬 Idioms

### “give it a try”

to attempt something you have not done before

- *I have never tried kayaking, but I would love to give it a try this summer.*
- *The recipe looks complicated, but let us give it a try anyway.*

### “pick it up quickly”

to learn a new skill in a short amount of time

- *She had never used this software before, but she picked it up quickly.*
- *Do not worry if you are a beginner — most people pick it up quickly with a little practice.*

### “no worries”

a phrase used to tell someone not to worry about a small mistake or inconvenience

- *'Sorry, I forgot to bring the book.' 'No worries, we can share mine.'*
- *She apologized for being late, and he replied, 'No worries, we just started.'*

### “move on to”

to finish one topic or activity and transition to the next one

- *We have covered the grammar point, so let us move on to the reading exercise.*
- *Once you finish this level, you can move on to the advanced course.*

## 📚 Vocabulary to Remember

- **pier** — a long platform built out over water where people can walk or boats can dock
  - *They watched the sunset standing at the end of the pier.*
- **relaxing** — calm and enjoyable; helping you feel less stressed or tired
  - *A quiet walk in the park can be very relaxing after a long day at work.*
- **irregular verb** — a verb whose simple past tense does not follow the standard -ed pattern (for example: go → went, eat → ate, take → took)
  - *It takes time to memorize every irregular verb in English.*
- **stand-up paddleboarding** — a water sport in which you stand on a wide board and use a long paddle to move across the water
  - *Stand-up paddleboarding looks difficult at first, but most beginners find their balance quickly.*
- **get confused** — to feel uncertain or mixed up about something
  - *It is easy to get confused when there are so many similar grammar rules.*

## 🔧 Say It Better

1. ❌ *I goed to the beach with my friend on Saturday.*
   ✅ **I went to the beach with my friend on Saturday.**
   💡 'Go' is an irregular verb; its simple past is 'went', not 'goed'.
2. ❌ *We was just walking and talking.*
   ✅ **We were just walking and talking.**
   💡 Use 'were' (not 'was') with the subject 'we'.
3. ❌ *walking and talking for long time*
   ✅ **walking and talking for a long time**
   💡 The phrase 'a long time' requires the article 'a' before 'long'.
4. ❌ *we eated some ice cream near the pier*
   ✅ **we ate some ice cream near the pier**
   💡 'Eat' is an irregular verb; its simple past is 'ate', not 'eated'.
5. ❌ *I taked a lot of pictures.*
   ✅ **I took a lot of pictures.**
   💡 'Take' is an irregular verb; its simple past is 'took', not 'taked'.
6. ❌ *my friend say is very fun*
   ✅ **my friend says it is very fun**
   💡 Third-person singular requires the -s ending ('says'), and the subject 'it' must not be dropped.
7. ❌ *someone who teach me*
   ✅ **someone who can teach me**
   💡 A relative clause expressing ability needs a modal verb: 'who can teach me'.
8. ❌ *I always confuse which verbs are irregular.*
   ✅ **I always get confused about which verbs are irregular.**
   💡 The natural phrasing is 'get confused about something' when describing the feeling of being uncertain.
